# Resistance-training evidence for content-bundle parameters

Date: 2026-09-23

## Scope

Question: what evidence-based parameters should a resistance-training app use for healthy adults
(general population, beginner and intermediate, goal hypertrophy or strength, 2–4 days/week)?

- Primary source: the ACSM 2026 Position Stand. It exists, and I read its full text on PMC. Its
  actual title is *"American College of Sports Medicine Position Stand. Resistance Training
  Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An
  Overview of Reviews"*. It is an overview (umbrella review) of 137 systematic reviews and says it
  updates the 2009 stand. I also read the full text of the 2009 stand, because the 2026 stand
  deliberately gives few numbers and the app needs numbers.
- Only primary sources were used: the papers themselves, through PubMed/PMC, publisher pages and
  DOIs. Where I could open only an abstract, the table says so. Any claim that depends on text I
  could not open is marked **unverified**.
- **About quotations.** A copyright policy applies to this work and allows at most one verbatim
  quotation in this document. I used it on the ACSM 2009 load-increment wording, which you asked me
  to check word for word (§6). Every other row gives a close paraphrase in my own words, with exact
  numbers and a locator (section, table or page), so each claim can be checked against the source.
- **Certainty grades are my own appraisal** and use this scale:
  - **High:** two or more independent syntheses agree, with a large pooled sample (>1,000) of
    mostly randomised trials.
  - **Moderate:** at least one meta-analysis agrees, but it has few studies (<15), a narrow
    population (for example young men or mostly untrained people) or some inconsistency.
  - **Low:** one small synthesis, an exploratory analysis, a narrative review, expert or panel
    opinion, a single RCT, or a claim I could verify only at abstract level.

  Where a source grades itself, I report that grade too. ACSM 2009 uses NHLBI categories A–D
  (A = rich RCT evidence; B = limited RCT evidence; C = non-randomised or observational;
  D = panel consensus; Table 1, p. 688). ACSM 2026 gives a quality-of-evidence percentage ("QoE")
  and a verdict for each variable in Tables 3–4: ✔ affects the outcome, ✖ does not, ? cannot tell.
- **Population caveat that applies everywhere.** ACSM 2026 says most of the evidence it
  synthesised comes from inexperienced (novice) trainees (Methods, "Eligibility Criteria"). Most
  pooled trials last 6–12 weeks. In general the literature does not separate "beginner" from
  "intermediate".

## 6. Load progression rule and maximum single-step increase

**Verified wording of the ACSM 2009 guidance.** ACSM09 Table 2 (p. 700), Evidence category B,
says that "a 2–10% increase in load be applied" once the lifter can already exceed the target by
one or two reps, **in two training sessions in a row**. The body text
(strength "Loading" subsection, p. 690) says the same. It also adds that the percentage should be
at the low end for small-muscle-mass exercises and at the high end for large-muscle-mass exercises,
and it limits the rule to people who train at a prescribed RM load.

The **Summary (p. 687) drops the two-session condition**. Versions quoted from the Summary
therefore omit it.

The rule's only citation is FEIGENBAUM99 (ref. 68), a narrative review. Its abstract does not
contain the rule, and I could not open its full text.

## Gaps and disagreements

1. **How specific a prescription should be.** ACSM26 deliberately moves away from fixed rep, rest
   and set criteria. It ranks individualisation for adherence above them (Discussion) and warns
   that the 2009 numbers may not suit most adults. ACSM09 gives specific numbers. The app needs
   numbers, so most of them trace back to ACSM09, whose grading ACSM26 criticises.
2. **Volume wording in ACSM26.** For strength, Table 6 expresses 2–3 sets per session, while the
   Discussion expresses about 2–3 sets per exercise. The weekly hypertrophy figures differ in
   kind: a floor of ≥10 sets per muscle (from included reviews) versus diminishing returns at about 18–20 (ACSM26's reading of
   PELLAND26, not verifiable in the PELLAND26 abstract).
3. **RIR.** ACSM26 proposes 2–3 RIR, yet says exact RIR targets cannot be quantified. ROBINSON24
   (exploratory, estimated RIR) favours closer to failure for hypertrophy only. REFALO23 finds a
   non-linear relationship with no identifiable optimum. No source addresses how accurately
   beginners report RIR. ACSM26 cites Halperin et al. 2022 on this; I did not read it.
4. **Rest.** ACSM09 recommends ≥2–3 min for core strength lifts, but ACSM26 found no effect of rest
   duration on strength. GRGIC18R splits the answer by training status. For hypertrophy, SINGER24
   finds a small benefit above 60 s where ACSM26 could not decide.
5. **Frequency for hypertrophy.** SCHOENFELD16F says twice weekly beats once weekly. SCHOENFELD19F,
   ACSM26 and PELLAND26 say frequency does not matter once volume is equal. ACSM26 notes that
   ACSM09's top-graded frequency advice rested on 3 studies (n = 59).
6. **Load increments.** The only numeric rule (ACSM09's 2–10%, 2 sessions) is Evidence B, rests on
   a single narrative review I could not verify, and appears without its two-session condition in
   the 2009 Summary. PLOTKIN22 shows that adding reps is an alternative in trained lifters.
7. **Stale history and gaps.** No source addresses when past performance stops counting for
   progression. The detraining evidence (BOSQUET13, ENCARNACAO22, IVERSEN21) concerns retaining
   adaptations and gives inconsistent, low-quality time courses.
8. **Warm-up and session time.** Neither ACSM stand addresses warm-up. No source gives minutes per
   set, per exercise or for warm-up.
9. **Population.** Much of the pooled evidence is from untrained, young, mostly male participants
   over 6–12 weeks (ACSM26, LOPEZ21, SINGER24; RALSTON17 and KASSIANO22 are men only). No source
   gives different parameters for "intermediate" beyond ACSM09's category definitions.
10. **ACSM09 internal grading inconsistency (minor).** The advanced hypertrophy loading
    recommendation is graded A in Table 2 (p. 700) but C in the body text (p. 694).
