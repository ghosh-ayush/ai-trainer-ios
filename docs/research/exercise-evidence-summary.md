## Method

Eight parallel literature sweeps (the papers cited in Jeff Nippard's exercise-science videos and articles, used only as pointers, plus independent PubMed/PMC searches by muscle region, use case and methodology). Each paper was read from PubMed/PMC or the publisher, findings were paraphrased with exact numbers, and a second agent re-opened every source to confirm, correct or reject each finding. Preprints (and findings whose numbers were read from a preprint of a published paper), animal and cadaver studies and practitioner opinion columns were removed. Exercise names were mapped to free-exercise-db ids, and two independent reviewers then checked each match against the catalog record (movement, implement, and any stance or angle the study varied); only matches both kept remain. Unmapped names keep an empty list.

## Read these findings with care

- muscleActivation is surface EMG amplitude from acute sessions; it is not evidence that an exercise grows or strengthens a muscle.
- Hip flexors are recorded under quadriceps and serratus anterior under shoulders, because the catalog has no finer muscle names.
- Certainty above low is allowed only for syntheses (meta-analyses, systematic and umbrella reviews, position stands).

## Gaps

- **lats: hypertrophy (and localEndurance)** — There are 32 muscleActivation findings but no human lat-specific hypertrophy trial. Of the 2 hypertrophy entries, one is a quail stretch-overload study (10.1152/jappl.1993.75.3.1263) and the other is a whole-body DXA fat-free-mass proxy (10.3389/fphys.2017.01105). localEndurance has 1 entry (velocity-loss pull-ups), and its between-group difference was not significant. As a result, no lat exercise can be ranked for growth from non-EMG evidence.
- **traps: hypertrophy, strength, localEndurance (in practice EMG-only)** — There are 24 muscleActivation entries. The single hypertrophy entry and the single localEndurance entry both come from an NSCA expert-prescription article on the upright row (10.1519/SSC.0b013e31822ec3e3), which contains no trial data. The single strength entry is the pooled weightlifting-movement load in Morris 2022 (10.1007/s40279-021-01627-2), where the traps tag is inferred. So there is no trial-based trap size or strength evidence at all.
- **middle back: hypertrophy and strength** — There are 17 muscleActivation and 10 posture entries (mostly clinical kyphosis and scapular programmes). The single hypertrophy entry is whole-body DXA. The 2 strength entries are a prone bench pull inside a free-weight vs machine comparison and a tempo-review citation. No rhomboid or mid-trapezius size trial with rows was found. My PubMed searches for rhomboid or middle-trapezius thickness after row training returned nothing, so this may be a real gap in the literature rather than a retrieval miss.
- **abductors: hypertrophy and strength** — There are 18 muscleActivation findings (side-lying abduction, clamshell and band walk EMG). Hypertrophy has 1 entry, an indirect one: gluteus medius and minimus barely grew after hip thrusts or squats. Strength has 1 entry, a side effect of Copenhagen adduction training. No trial trained hip abduction directly and measured gluteus medius size or strength, so the EMG rankings are the only basis for choosing an abductor exercise.
- **abdominals: hypertrophy and strength** — There are 53 muscleActivation entries. Of the 4 hypertrophy entries, one is an expert argument with no trial (10.1519/SSC.0b013e3182259d05), one is a soccer stabilisation MRI study with no group effect, one is rectus abdominis CSA from a free-weight vs machine trial, and one is whole-body DXA. One of the 3 strength entries is expert advice from the same NSCA article. No trial directly compares abdominal exercises such as the crunch, the roll-out and hanging raises for growth or strength.
- **forearms: hypertrophy, localEndurance, power** — Hypertrophy rests on 1 finding (a handgrip muscle-thickness study with no measurement site). Strength has 4 entries, and two of them are grip results pooled from a circuit meta-analysis or a periodised programme. There is no localEndurance or power evidence. Wrist curls, reverse curls and grip work cannot be ranked from outcome data.
- **calves: strength and localEndurance** — Calves have 15 hypertrophy findings but only 1 strength finding (light vs heavy isometric plantarflexion) and no localEndurance evidence. Standing vs seated raises, foot position and ROM are compared only on thickness.
- **hamstrings: eccentric-strength evidence for the Nordic hamstring exercise, and localEndurance** — The only Nordic training trial is Bourne 2017. The widely cited Nordic dose-response and meta-analytic evidence on eccentric knee-flexor strength (and biceps femoris long head fascicle length) is missing. Hamstring localEndurance has 0 entries.
- **programming variables: weekly volume (hypertrophy and strength)** — Volume evidence comes only from the 2025 Pelland meta-regression and a SportRxiv preprint (10.51224/SRXIV.537). The foundational primary and meta-analytic volume papers are absent, including the one trained-men RCT showing that volume affected hypertrophy but not strength.
- **programming variables: frequency, rest interval, failure, load, progression** — Frequency for strength and the updated frequency-hypertrophy meta-analysis are missing. The Schoenfeld 2016 frequency meta-analysis is listed as unreadable yet still contributes a finding. The only primary trial of inter-set rest in trained men is missing, as are the Morton load/hormone trial and the Grgic failure meta-analysis. Lasevicius 2018 appears only as a secondary citation, and the load vs repetition progression trial is also missing. Most of these would yield findings with no muscle tag, so they help the rule engine but not muscle mapping.
- **biceps/triceps: adding single-joint work to multi-joint programmes** — Whether curls and extensions add anything to presses and pulls is central to exercise selection, and the evidence here conflicts: Gentil 2015 and Mannarino 2021 disagree. The companion RCT that tested adding single-joint exercises is missing.
- **shoulders: rotator cuff and serratus strength (no vocabulary term)** — Shoulder strength evidence is mostly pressing 1RM. Rotator cuff and serratus anterior appear only as EMG findings (e.g., 10.1111/sae.12010, 10.2519/jospt.2003.33.5.247), and serratus is force-mapped to shoulders. There is no training evidence for external-rotation strength.
- **upper-body power (chest/triceps/shoulders)** — Chest, triceps and shoulders have only 2-3 power findings each. These are either cross-sectional correlations (shot put vs bench 1RM; pectoral volume vs bench-throw power) or a null low-load push-up trial. No training trial of ballistic upper-body work (bench throw, medicine-ball throw) was found. A PubMed search for such training RCTs returned nothing. What exists is acute potentiation evidence, which is not a training adaptation.
- **localEndurance across most muscles** — Several muscles have no localEndurance evidence: hamstrings, calves, adductors, abductors and forearms. Biceps, lats and traps have 1 entry each, and the traps entry is expert opinion. Many existing localEndurance entries record acute within-session rep counts rather than training adaptations: reps at 10RM after pre-exhaustion (10.1519/1533-4287(2003)017<0411:eopeol>2.0.co;2), reps lost after pre-set stretching (10.1007/s00421-016-3527-3), and external-focus reps (10.1080/1750984X.2012.723728). These should be separated from chronic endurance outcomes.
- **cardiorespiratory mapping to exercises** — Every muscle's single cardiorespiratory finding comes from one soccer pre-season trial (multi-joint vs single-joint, 10.3389/fphys.2017.01105). All other cardiorespiratory findings (circuit, HIFT, concurrent-training and older-adult metas) have an empty muscle field. As a result, no exercise-level cardiorespiratory recommendation can be derived.
- **evidence-type hygiene (inflated counts)** — Several entries are counted as outcomes but are not training evidence. Expert-opinion NSCA articles are tagged hypertrophy, strength or localEndurance (10.1519/SSC.0b013e3182259d05, 10.1519/SSC.0b013e31822ec3e3, 10.1519/SSC.0b013e3182221ec2). An animal study is counted as lat hypertrophy (10.1152/jappl.1993.75.3.1263), and a cadaver moment-arm study is tagged strength for the neck (10.1016/j.jbiomech.2010.09.036). A static MRI position study is tagged posture (8222458). Acute 1RM or 6RM load capacity is tagged strength (10.1519/JSC.0b013e318276b873, 10.1097/JSC.0000000000000232). Cross-sectional correlations are tagged strength (10.1519/JSC.0000000000000306, 10.1080/02640414.2024.2430855). Barbalho 2020 (10.1055/a-1082-1126) carries published data-integrity concerns. A number of 'unreadable' papers (e.g., Zabaleta-Korta 2021, Mannarino 2021, Pedrosa 2022, Kassiano 2023, Varovic 2025, Martinez-Cava 2022, Calatayud 2015, Pinto 2025, Barbalho 2020, Pallares 2020, Robinson 2024 preprint values) still supply findings, which should be flagged as abstract-only or partial. Lopez 2021 values are marked pre-corrigendum and need rechecking against the corrigendum.
- **muscle vocabulary gaps causing mis-attribution** — Hip flexors (iliopsoas, sartorius) and rectus femoris-as-hip-flexor are mapped to quadriceps in the sit-up and leg-raise EMG findings (e.g., 10.1007/s004210050135, 10.1093/ptj/86.5.656). Serratus anterior is mapped to shoulders. Tibialis anterior and posterior, flexor digitorum longus and peroneus longus are mapped to calves (10.1016/j.ptsp.2017.08.077, 10.1097/00005768-200204000-00019). Tensor fasciae latae is mapped to abductors. As a result, activation counts for quadriceps and calves include muscles that the exercises in question do not target.

## Contradictions between studies

- Lat pulldown bar position: the primary Sperandei 2009 paper (10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181b8d30a; PubMed abstract PMID 19855327 re-checked) found no lat EMG difference between behind-neck, front and V-bar pulldowns. The review 10.3390/jfmk11030315 credits the same study with a front-over-behind concentric lat advantage. The review's secondary claim should not override the primary result.
- Pulldown grip width and orientation: Signorile 2002 (10.1519/1533-4287(2002)016<0539:ACEIOM>2.0.CO;2) found a wide front grip beat all other grips for lat EMG. Andersen 2014 (10.1097/JSC.0000000000000232) found no whole-rep grip-width difference, with wide above narrow only eccentrically. Lusk 2010 (10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181ddb0ab) found pronated beat supinated, whereas Lehman 2004 (10.1186/1476-5918-3-4) found no significant wide-pronated vs supinated difference (26.2 vs 22.4 %MVC).
- Incline vs flat pressing for the upper pectoral, EMG: Trebs 2010 (10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181ddfae7) found 44 and 56 degrees above flat. Barnett 1995 (10.1519/00124278-199511000-00003) found incline no higher than flat. Rodriguez-Ridao 2020 (10.3390/ijerph17197339) found 0-45 degrees did not differ pairwise, despite its abstract claim. Lauver 2016 (10.1080/17461391.2015.1022605) found differences only in the 26-50% portion of the concentric phase.
- Incline vs flat pressing, growth: Chaves (10.70252/FDNB1158) found incline grew only the upper pectoral site more, with mid and lower sites similar. The variation review (10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258) summarises incline as beating flat for pectoralis thickness overall (+55-62% vs +32-43%). Pinto 2025 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000005045) found no region-specific pectoral growth with the pec deck.
- Rectus femoris growth from squats: 10.3390/ijerph192316226 found 7 weeks of high-bar squats grew rectus femoris like the other heads. Kubo 2019 (10.1007/s00421-019-04181-y) found rectus femoris volume unchanged after full or half squats. Maeo 2024 (10.1249/MSS.0000000000003957) found only +1.1% (non-significant) after leg press. Zabaleta-Korta 2021 (10.1080/02640414.2021.1929736) found Smith squats grew only central vastus lateralis. The Vigotsky review (10.3389/fphys.2017.00985) cites squat trials showing no rectus femoris growth.
- Overhead vs neutral-arm triceps extension: Maeo 2023 (10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279) found overhead grew the long head more (+28.5% vs +19.6%, MRI). Stasinaki 2018 (10.3390/jfmk3020028) found overhead and push-downs did not differ. The single-study summary in 10.1016/j.smhs.2025.03.001 even shows the push-down ahead proximally (14% vs 0%).
- Partial vs full ROM for triceps: Goto 2019 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000002051) found a 45-90 degree partial ROM beat full ROM (CSA +48.7% vs +28.2%). Wolf 2025 (10.7717/peerj.18904) found lengthened partials equal to full ROM. The Kassiano 2023 mid-range partial advantage (ES 1.21, 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004415) probably re-counts the same Goto trial, so it is not independent support.
- Full vs partial ROM meta-analyses: Pallares 2021 (10.1111/sms.14006) found full ROM gave more lower-limb hypertrophy (ES 0.88). Wolf 2023 (10.47206/ijsc.v3i1.182) found no overall size difference (SMD 0.04). At trial level, Bloomquist 2013 (deep beat shallow, 10.1007/s00421-013-2642-7) conflicts with Kubo 2019 (knee extensors 4.9% vs 4.6%) and with the leg-press depth trial 10.1080/02640414.2025.2481534 (similar gains).
- Training frequency for hypertrophy: Schoenfeld 2016 (10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8) found twice weekly beat once weekly with volume equated (ES 0.49 vs 0.30, P=0.002). Pelland 2025 (10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) found the frequency effect inconsistent and possibly negligible once weekly volume is modelled (0.32% per session, CrI -0.14 to 0.82).
- Load and hypertrophy: Fry 2004 (10.2165/00007256-200434100-00004) proposes 80-95% 1RM as optimal, and Campos 2002 (10.1007/s00421-002-0681-6) found no significant fibre growth with 20-28RM. The Schoenfeld 2017 meta-analysis (10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200), Lopez 2021 NMA (10.1249/MSS.0000000000002585), Mitchell 2012 (10.1152/japplphysiol.00307.2012) and Schoenfeld 2015 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000000958) all found similar hypertrophy across loads when sets reach failure.
- Do pulling exercises grow the biceps as well as curls? Gentil 2015 (10.5812/asjsm.24057) found lat-pulldown-only matched curl-only for elbow-flexor thickness (+6.10% vs +5.83%). Mannarino 2021 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000003234) found curls doubled row-trained growth (11.06% vs 5.16%, p=0.009).
- Squat vs hip thrust for glute growth: Barbalho 2020 (10.1055/a-1082-1126, with data-integrity concerns) found the squat grew glutes more than the hip thrust (+9.4% vs +3.7%). Plotkin 2023 (10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170) found similar MRI growth, slightly favouring the hip thrust. Acute EMG (10.1123/jab.2014-0301) strongly favoured the hip thrust. Maeo 2024 (10.1249/MSS.0000000000003957) found the leg press grew gluteus maximus 15.4%, so a large EMG advantage did not predict the growth ranking.
- Squat depth and glute EMG: Caterisano 2002 (10.1519/1533-4287(2002)016<0428:TEOBSD>2.0.CO;2) found the glute share rose with depth (16.9% to 35.4%). The glute review 32132843 cites a load-matched study where partial squats beat full squats, and another with no parallel-vs-full difference. For training, Kubo 2019 favours full depth for glute growth (6.7% vs 2.2%).
- Foot position in calf raises: Nunes 2020 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000003674) found toes-out favoured medial and toes-in favoured lateral gastrocnemius growth, and Riemann 2011 EMG (10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181cc22b8) agrees. However, the 45-degree leg-press calf-raise study (10.17784/mtprehabjournal.2017.15.529) found no foot-position effect on any head (P=0.75). Akuzawa (28515978) found medial gastrocnemius activity barely changed with foot position, and toes-in produced no head difference.
- Knee angle and calf EMG: 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31824435cf reports soleus about 4% higher and gastrocnemius about 5% lower with the knee bent. 10.1016/s1050-6411(03)00058-0 found no EMG change across knee angles of 60-180 degrees. For training, Kinoshita 2023 (10.3389/fphys.2023.1272106) found seated raises did not grow soleus more than standing raises (2.9% vs 2.1%, p=0.410).
- Roll-out and Ab-Slide for rectus abdominis: Escamilla 2006 (10.1093/ptj/86.5.656) and 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31818745bf rank roll-out devices highest for abdominal EMG. Sternlicht 2004 (15085210) found the ABslide gave the lowest upper rectus abdominis EMG (148 vs 213 for the trunk curl), though it used non-normalised EMG.
- Static stretching before sets: 10.1007/s00421-016-3527-3 found pre-set stretching blunted vastus lateralis growth (7.4% vs 12.7%). 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003283 found no effect on biceps femoris growth when stretching preceded leg curls. Inter-set stretching boosted vastus lateralis in 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003036 but gave no pectoral or quadriceps benefit in the trials summarised by 10.3389/fspor.2022.1035190.
- Training at long muscle lengths: lengthening via joint position favoured longer lengths in three trials, namely seated vs prone leg curl (10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523), overhead triceps (10.1080/17461391.2022.2100279) and reclined leg extension (10.1080/02640414.2024.2444713). Resistance-profile bias with ROM matched showed no effect in any of four muscles (10.51224/SRXIV.660), and cable-curl shoulder position was null (10.3389/fphys.2026.1750722). Yet incline vs preacher curls found region-specific differences (10.1055/a-2517-0509).
- Free weights vs machines for strength: Haugen 2023 (10.1249/MSS.0000000000003271) found similar gains, with each group best in its own modality. The meta-analysis 10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4 found free weights better when testing was on free weights, and machines better for upper-body strength tested on each group's own modality (SMD 0.245).
- Core training and jumping: the meta-analysis 10.5114/biolsport.2023.123319 reports core training improves vertical jump (SMD 0.69). Prieske 2016 (10.1111/sms.12403) did not report countermovement-jump improvement. Hoshikawa (10.1519/JSC.0b013e31828bed36) found jump gains only within the stabilisation group, with no between-group sprint effect.
- Hip thrust and sprinting: Contreras 2017 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000001510) found possibly larger 10-20 m sprint gains than with front squats. The review 31191088 reports that 2 of 4 hip-thrust training studies found no sprint improvement vs control.
- Leg curl vs hip hinge for hamstrings: Schoenfeld 2015 (10.1519/JSC.0000000000000598) found the lying leg curl beat the stiff-leg deadlift at distal sites. The deadlift review (10.1371/journal.pone.0229507) reports the same Schoenfeld sample both as favouring the leg curl and as favouring the stiff-leg deadlift for upper hamstrings, and cites McAllister finding the Romanian deadlift above the leg curl for biceps femoris.
- Inverted row and lats: the primary Fenwick 2009 finding (10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181942019) shows the inverted row's advantage only on the left latissimus, which was the non-pulling side for the one-arm cable row, with no exercise effect on the right lat. The review 10.3390/jfmk11030315 summarises the inverted row as giving the highest lat EMG outright.
- Internal inconsistencies within single sources: in Coratella 2022 (10.3389/fphys.2022.825880) the conclusion denies an anterior-deltoid difference that its results report. Sperandei 2009's abstract and results disagree on eccentric pectoral effects. In Rodriguez-Ridao 2020 the abstract claims a 30-degree advantage that its pairwise tests do not support. Robinson 2024 values come from the preprint, not the published version.
