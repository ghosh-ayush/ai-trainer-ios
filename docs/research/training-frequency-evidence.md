# Training-frequency evidence for plans of 1–7 sessions per week

Date: 2026-09-24

## Scope

Question: when an athlete has anywhere from 1 to 7 free days a week and a limit on session time,
what does the peer-reviewed evidence say about how to spread resistance training across those days?

- **Builds on** `resistance-training-evidence.md` (2026-09-23). Source keys from that file are
  reused unchanged. Its findings are not repeated unless a frequency question needs them.
- **Qualifying sources only.** Every source is a peer-reviewed publication with a DOI or PMID and
  one of these designs: position stand, umbrella review, meta-analysis, systematic review,
  randomised or crossover trial, cohort study or narrative review. No preprint is cited or used
  anywhere in this file.
- **Certainty.** The grades are my own appraisal, on the repository scale:
  - **High:** two or more independent syntheses agree, the pooled sample is large (>1,000) and
    most studies are randomised trials.
  - **Moderate:** at least one meta-analysis agrees, but it has few studies (<15), a narrow
    population or some inconsistency.
  - **Low:** one small synthesis, an exploratory analysis, a narrative review, panel opinion, a
    single RCT, or a claim I could verify only at abstract level.

  Two further limits apply. First, only a synthesis (meta-analysis, systematic review, umbrella
  review or position stand) can be graded above Low. A single RCT, crossover trial, cohort study
  or narrative review is Low at most, however well it was done. Second, a position-stand
  statement that its authors call expert consensus counts as panel opinion, so it is graded Low.
- **Identifiers checked.** On 2026-09-24 every DOI below resolved through the doi.org handle API,
  and its Crossref title matched the paper named. Every PMID was fetched from PubMed on the same
  day and checked for a "Retracted Publication" type and for retraction or expression-of-concern
  notices. None of the sources cited carries one.
- **Locators.** Every number that could become an app parameter has a locator (section, table,
  figure or page). For sources I could open only at abstract level, the locator is "Abstract".
  A number I could not see in text I opened is marked **unverified**.
- **Quotations.** None. Every row is a close paraphrase with exact numbers.
- **"Owner decision"** means the literature gives no number. The rationale is then built only
  from findings cited here.
- **Access to the four central frequency papers (attempted again on 2026-09-24).**
  - PELLAND26: the Springer page showed the abstract and marked the rest as subscription
    content. Europe PMC lists it as "Subscription required", and there is no PMC copy.
    **Abstract only.**
  - SCHOENFELD16F and GRGIC18F: the Springer pages showed only the abstract and reference list
    (subscription). There is no PMC copy. **Abstract only.**
  - SCHOENFELD19F: the Taylor & Francis page showed a human-verification challenge, which I did
    not attempt to pass. There is no PMC or Europe PMC free copy. **Abstract only.** IUSCA21
    (full text, p. 9) reports its frequency categories, and I cite that where it matters.
- **Population caveat.** Most frequency trials in trained people studied young men for 6–12
  weeks. Most pooled evidence in ACSM26 comes from novices (see the earlier file).

## Gaps and disagreements

1. **Per-session ceiling is unquantified.** IUSCA21 (p. 10) proposes about 10 sets per muscle per
   session but says the threshold has not been quantified. Randomised trials in trained men
   showed no loss at about 15–18 direct sets per session (SCHOENFELD19V, LASEVICIUS19,
   BRIGATTO22V). One small trial in untrained men favoured spreading sets for strength (OCHI18).
   No synthesis addresses it. This single gap decides how much a 1- or 2-day week can hold.
2. **The weekly plateau is unverified.** ACSM26's "about 18–20 weekly sets" (Discussion) cites
   PELLAND26, whose full text I could not open. Its abstract gives no number. BAZVALLE22 (6 studies,
   trained men) found no benefit above 20 for quadriceps and biceps but did for triceps.
   SCHOENFELD19V and BRIGATTO22V found gains up to 30–45 and 32 weekly sets.
3. **Indirect-set counting disagrees.** PELLAND26 (abstract) found counting indirect sets as 0.5
   fitted best. SCHOENFELD19S defaults to 1:1. BAZVALLE22 counted multi-joint sets fully for the
   triceps. Whether the 18–20 figure is in fractional sets is unknown.
4. **Hypertrophy and frequency.** SCHOENFELD16F (twice beats once) is contradicted by the larger
   SCHOENFELD19F, ACSM26 and PELLAND26 (no effect at equal volume).
5. **Strength and frequency.** ACSM26 (≥2 d/wk; Table 6), GRGIC18F (rising ES), PELLAND26 (positive,
   with diminishing returns) and KNEFFEL21 (+0.14 ES per day) support more sessions. RALSTON18F and
   CUTHBERT21 find no clear effect once volume is equated. RALSTON18F reports a data-entry error in
   GRGIC18F.
6. **CUTHBERT21 is internally inconsistent.** The Results text reports lower-body p = 0.453 and
   g = 0.088, but Table 1 gives g = 0.061 and p = 0.651 (0.453 is the Z value). The Discussion's
   opening paragraph repeats the Table 1 values. I used Table 1.
7. **The 48 h recovery rule has no verified qualifying source.** It reaches the syntheses through
   ACSM guidance (DANKEL17F abstract; CUTHBERT21, citing a textbook). One 5 d/wk trial (GOMES19,
   Monday–Friday per CARNEIRO24) trained every muscle on consecutive days at about 2–3 direct sets
   per session without worse outcomes. The other 5 d/wk trials and the 6 d/wk trials did not report
   which days, so their spacing is unknown. No study compared intervals.
8. **7 days/week and rest days are not tested.** No qualifying trial of conventional 7 d/wk
   training exists, and no source sets a number of rest days. GRANDOU20 (abstract) gives only a
   qualitative link between overtraining and frequent, high-intensity, monotonous training.
9. **Split evidence is thin at 5–7 days.** It comes from young trained men over 4–10 weeks. The
   5-day evidence is three trials, not four: CARNEIRO24 reports the GOMES19 participants.
   RAMOSCAMPO24 is abstract-only. Push/pull/legs is not verifiably tested, and RIBEIRO15 is a
   10-person pilot.
10. **Once weekly vs strength guidance.** ACSM26 (Table 6) recommends ≥2 sessions for strength,
    while RALSTON18F finds once weekly equal at equal volume. For older adults, TAAFFE99 and
    KNEFFEL21 suggest 1–2 sessions suffice for strength, but BICKEL11 shows older adults lose size
    at a once-weekly maintenance dose.
11. **Session minutes.** No source gives minutes per set. The only figure (about 1.9 min per set at
    90 s rest) is my arithmetic from SCHOENFELD19V, and it excludes warm-up.
12. **Population.** Frequency trials in trained people are almost all in young men. PEDERSEN22 is
    the only structure trial in women, and it studied untrained women. KNEFFEL21 and TAAFFE99 cover
    older adults.
13. **Adherence.** PEDERSEN22's time-related dropouts in the 4-day group are the only
    frequency–adherence data found. No synthesis addresses adherence by frequency.
14. **ACSM09 body text** (48 h, advanced 4–6-day splits) was not reopened. Its locators here come
    from the earlier file or its Summary only.

## Suggested design for 1–7 days/week

These rules apply to every row. Each is an owner decision unless a key is given.

- **Weekly target per major muscle:** hypertrophy 10–20 working sets (floor ≥10: ACSM26 Table 6,
  IUSCA21 p. 9; upper end: BAZVALLE22 Conclusions, and ACSM26's unverified reading of PELLAND26).
  The time-limited floor is ≥4 (IVERSEN21 Table 1). For strength, 2–3 sets per main lift per
  session on ≥2 sessions/week (ACSM26 Table 6).
- **Counting:** a set counts 1 for its target muscle and 0.5 for a synergist. *Owner decision*
  based on PELLAND26's best-fitting model (Abstract; Low).
- **Per-session cap: 10 working sets per muscle.** *Owner decision.* It adopts the only published
  number (IUSCA21 p. 10, Low), which is conservative against RCTs showing 15–18 sets per session
  work in trained men (§3).
- **Consecutive-day rule (C):** if two sessions that train the same muscle fall on consecutive
  days, the later one gives that muscle ≤4 working sets, stopped short of failure. *Owner
  decision.* The only consecutive-day protocol verified in this file (GOMES19, Monday–Friday per
  CARNEIRO24) used about 2–3 direct sets per muscle per session (1–2 sets per exercise). FRANCO21
  reached about 2 per session but its weekdays are not reported. The value 4 is therefore one set
  above the highest tested dose and is an untested owner choice. Failure slowed recovery for up to
  24–48 h after the session in trained men (MORANNAVARRO17; Low).
- **Spacing rule (S):** otherwise, sessions giving a muscle more than 4 sets are ≥48 h apart when
  the free days allow. *Owner decision.* That interval matches tested designs (PEDERSEN22;
  LASEVICIUS19 full body; SCHOENFELD19V) and the ~48 h protein-synthesis window (IUSCA21 p. 9).
  No study shows it is required (§5).
- **Time budget:** sets per session ≈ (minutes − warm-up) ÷ 2 at about 90 s rest. *Owner
  computation* from SCHOENFELD19V (about 1.9 min per set). That figure applies to 8–12-rep sets to
  failure with 90 s between sets and about 120 s between exercises, and no warm-up is reported.
  Longer rests, such as the ≥2 min IUSCA21 advises for multi-joint lifts (Table 1, p. 22), make each
  set take longer. If the weekly target does not fit, fall back toward the ≥4 floor (IVERSEN21) and
  keep the multi-joint roles.
